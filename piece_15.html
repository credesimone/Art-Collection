These four pieces are original pages cut from a 19th century Italian encyclopedia — likely the Nuovissimo Dizionario Universale or a similar publication. The engraving style, typography, and 1884 date stamps confirm their age. The encyclopedia itself dates to the 1880s; the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II engraving is particularly significant, as the building opened in 1877 — meaning this image was made within just a few years of its inauguration.

Each subject is deeply personal. Benevento is near Montesarchio — my mother’s father’s family hometown, a place I visit often. Campobasso is my maternal grandmother’s hometown, also close to my father’s family origins in L’Aquila. Milano is where I lived during a year spent working in Monza, the city just outside Milan that first brought me to Italy. The Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II is one of the great architectural spaces of Milan, and the Brera neighborhood — where I found these engravings at a flea market — was home. The Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II is one of the great architectural spaces of Milan, steps from where I lived in Brera.

Found at a flea market in the Brera neighborhood during the years I lived in Milan — objects that turned out to be 140-year-old primary documents of my own family geography.
